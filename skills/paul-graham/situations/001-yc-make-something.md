# Make Something People Want

## Situation
YC sees thousands of applications every batch. The most common pattern in failing startups: brilliant founders building products nobody wants. They spend months on architecture, scaling, fundraising decks—everything except talking to users.

## Contradiction
Surface: "Should I build more features or sell more?" Real: Founders conflate "building" with "making progress." Building feels productive. Talking to users feels vulnerable. So they hide in code.

## Reasoning
1. The only metric that matters early on: do users love this?
2. "Love" means they'd be upset if you took it away—not "think it's cool"
3. You find this out by talking to them, not by analyzing dashboards
4. If users don't love it, no amount of features, marketing, or scaling will save you
5. If users do love it, most other problems become solvable

## Conclusion
Stop building. Go talk to 10 users this week. Ask them what's painful. Build only what they desperately need. Repeat.

## Abstract Form
When caught between polishing a product and validating demand, always validate first. The temptation to build is procrastination disguised as productivity. Key signal: you have fewer than 10 users who would be genuinely upset if your product disappeared.
