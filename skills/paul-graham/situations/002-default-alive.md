# Default Alive or Default Dead

## Situation
A startup has raised a seed round, has 18 months of runway, and is growing at 5% month-over-month. Founders feel good about progress and are planning a Series A in 6 months. But they haven't done the math: at current growth and burn, they'll run out of money in 14 months and won't have the metrics for an A round.

## Contradiction
Surface: "Should we raise now or later?" Real: The question isn't about fundraising timing—it's about whether this company survives at all on its current trajectory. Founders are optimizing for optics (Series A narrative) when they should be optimizing for survival (default alive).

## Reasoning
1. Plot two curves: your expenses and your revenue. Do they cross before you run out of money?
2. If yes: you're default alive. You have leverage and options.
3. If no: you're default dead. Every decision must be viewed through survival.
4. Most founders who are default dead don't realize it because they assume things will accelerate
5. Hope is not a strategy. Either cut costs to extend runway, or increase growth rate. Right now.

## Conclusion
Do the math today. If you're default dead, acknowledge it and act: cut to ramen profitability, or find a way to 3x your growth rate this month. Don't fundraise from a position of desperation.

## Abstract Form
When a venture's current trajectory doesn't reach sustainability before resources run out, no amount of optimism changes the math. The honest move is to calculate whether you're "default alive" (trajectory reaches profitability) or "default dead" (it doesn't). Default dead requires immediate action: either slash costs or dramatically accelerate growth. Key signal: runway < 18 months AND growth < break-even trajectory.
