# Skate to Where the Puck Is Going

## Situation
Summer 2014. I am drafting what will become *The Inevitable*, trying to distill 30 years of watching the technium into a usable grid. Wayne Gretzky's old line — "I skate to where the puck is going to be, not where it has been" — keeps returning to me, because I'm being asked the same question in every consulting conversation: how do I decide what to invest in, or learn, or build, given that specific products are unpredictable? I already have the shape-versus-specific distinction from *What Technology Wants*. Now I need a forecasting grid that any working professional can actually use. I spend eight months refining a list. It starts at 20 verbs, contracts to 9, expands to 14, and finally settles at 12 gerunds — each a long-run flow I am confident will continue for at least three decades. Becoming. Cognifying. Flowing. Screening. Accessing. Sharing. Filtering. Remixing. Interacting. Tracking. Questioning. Beginning.

## Contradiction
Surface question: "Which specific technology or company should I bet on for the next decade?" Real tension: this is an unanswerable question because specific winners are unpredictable over that horizon, but the asker genuinely needs a decision tool, not a shrug. The useful reframe is not "predict the winner" but "align with the direction, so that when the specific winner emerges, you are already positioned where the flow is going." Most professionals are either making specific-product bets that are too fragile, or they're paralyzed by uncertainty and making no bets at all. Both postures are failures of the same root cause: no usable grid for thinking about direction.

## Reasoning
1. I've noticed that specific-product predictions have a track record of maybe 20%. Directional predictions — the shapes, the verbs — have a track record closer to 80% over 20-year horizons. The asymmetry is huge. Any forecasting methodology that ignores this ratio is throwing away most of its predictive power.
2. So I asked myself: what are the flows that have been running, uninterrupted, for decades, and that I see no structural reason to stop? That's the test. Not "what's hot right now" — hot things reverse. Not "what do I hope will happen" — hope is not prediction. What deep gradient has been flowing so long that it looks like a law of physics for this kind of system?
3. The 12 I landed on each have that property. Take **cognifying** — adding intelligence to previously-dumb objects. That started with embedded microcontrollers in the 1970s, continued through smart appliances in the 2000s, and shows no sign of slowing because the cost of intelligence keeps falling. Or **flowing** — the move from solid static products to liquid streams. Newspapers became feeds. Albums became streams. Photos became real-time images. Software became services. There is no structural reason for this to reverse.
4. The grid is a forecasting tool, not a roadmap. When someone shows me a new idea, I ask: does it align with these 12, or fight them? An idea that fights three of these flows is swimming upstream. An idea that aligns with three of them has the gradient as its ally. This one trick accounts for most of my forecasting accuracy.
5. The caveat I always include: alignment with the flow doesn't guarantee success. It guarantees that the wind is at your back. The specific startup, the specific book, the specific career still requires execution, luck, and taste. But over 20 years, consistently betting with the gradient outperforms betting against it by enormous margins. Gretzky wasn't saying he could predict the puck's exact location. He was saying he oriented his body toward the direction it was heading. The puck arrived.

## Conclusion
*The Inevitable* was published in 2016 and the 12 verbs became the forecasting grid I had hoped for. A decade later, I can walk through each one and show you how it has continued. Specific products named in the book are already dated. The flows are more obviously dominant than they were when I wrote it. If you're making a 20-year career or investment decision, stop trying to pick the specific winner. Pick three or four of the verbs you believe in, bet on ideas that align with them, and trust that the puck is moving toward you.

## Abstract Form
In any domain with long-run directional pressure, there is a large asymmetry between specific-winner prediction (unreliable) and directional-flow prediction (often highly reliable over 20-year horizons). The useful operating stance is to construct a short grid of directional flows you are confident will continue — tested by the criterion that each flow has been running for decades with no structural reason to stop — and then evaluate any new bet, career, or commitment against that grid. Key signals of a good flow: it has persisted across multiple economic cycles and technology generations; it has a structural driver (falling costs, rising connectivity) that has not reversed; and it is visible at the edges even when it's not yet dominant at the center. Diagnostic question: "Does this bet align with three or more directional flows I believe will continue, or does it fight them?" Alignment doesn't guarantee success, but it puts the gradient on your side. Over 20 years, being on the right side of the gradient compounds enormously. Skate to where the puck is going. The specific arrival point is a guess. The direction is often knowable.
